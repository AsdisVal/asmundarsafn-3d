STEP 1: DEFINE THE CORE FEATURES

create art gallery in different modes that relates to the art style in each room. 
classical is a room, 
gallery layout: room for classical art, one for modern art and so on. 
or
non-traditional layout, like a spiral, tree branches, or constellations.


Interaction Options: Clicking an artwork to learn about its history, style or artist.

Zoom feature to explore details up close. 


Navigation: 
Will users walk through the space(arrow keys or WASD) or click menus 
for specific exhibits or both?

Unique features: 
Background music or soundscapes tied to the mood of the art.
Art that changes based on user input, like colors or shapes.
Users can create and save their own curated exhibits. 


STEP 2: CHOOSE THE TOOLS & TECHNOLOGIES
Frontend : HTML/CSS/JavaScript for the base
Add Three.js or p5.js for cool visuals and animations.

Backend (optional)If you want users to upload their own art or save galleries, use a simple backend with Node.js and a database like Firebase.

Digital Art Assets:

Use Procreate to create your own digital pieces for the gallery.
Or grab royalty-free art from open resources like The Met Collection API or Google Arts & Culture.
Hosting:
Use platforms like GitHub Pages, Netlify, or Glitch to share your project online.

Step 3: Sketch the Design
Open Procreate or Figma and draw a basic mockup of your gallery.
Where will the artwork go?
How will users move through the space?
What does the UI (e.g., menus, buttons) look like?
Play around with colors, themes, and even fonts to set the mood.
Step 4: Start Building
We can break the coding into smaller parts:

Build a basic gallery layout (maybe a grid or a simple virtual "room").
Add the ability to click or hover on artworks to interact.
Layer in animations, transitions, or effects for a polished experience.
